# Text+Graph Fusion with LMs: (A) Molecule / Chemistry Models and (B) LLM Graph-Reasoning Benchmarks (2023–2026)

Scope note: notes compiled 2026-11-02 from arXiv HTML/abs pages, conference pages and project pages. Every numeric claim below is attributed to the page it was read from. Where the arXiv page did not state the venue, the venue is taken from a second source (named) or marked "unverified". Several papers were read only through their first ~100k characters of HTML; remaining tables were not inspected and this is flagged where relevant.

## Key Question A1 — Catalogue of molecule models that fuse a graph encoder with an LM (MolCA, GIT-Mol, MoMu, MolT5, MoleculeSTM, 3D-MoLM, InstructMol, LLaMo, MolX, UniMoT, and 2025–2026 follow-ups)

### Takeaway
The dominant design (2023–2025) is a frozen or lightly tuned 2D GIN (usually the MoleculeSTM/GraphCL 5-layer GIN, hidden 300) feeding a frozen-ish decoder LM (Galactica-125M/1.3B, Vicuna/Llama-2-7B, Mistral-7B, Llama-3.1-8B) via a Q-Former (MolCA, 3D-MoLM, UniMoT, Mol-LLM, GIT-Mol), a linear/MLP projector (InstructMol, MolX), or a multi-level cross-attention projector (LLaMo); almost all of them also give the LM the SMILES/SELFIES string, so the graph is an *addition* to, not a replacement for, the string. Only 3D-MoLM uses a 3D encoder (Uni-Mol).

### Cited Findings

**Contrastive-alignment era (no generative LM; 2022–2023)**

- **MolT5 — "Translation between Molecules and Natural Language"** (Edwards, Lai, Ros, Honke, Cho, Ji; EMNLP 2022). Text-only: a T5 pretrained on unlabeled natural-language text and "molecule strings"; tasks = molecule captioning and text-based de novo molecule generation; no graph encoder. Serves as the SMILES-only baseline for most later papers. — [arXiv 2204.11817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11817)
- **MoMu — "A Molecular Multimodal Foundation Model Associating Molecule Graphs with Natural Language"** (Su, Du, Yang, Zhou, Li, Rao, Sun, Lu, Wen; arXiv 2022, no peer-reviewed venue on the page). Graph encoder: 5-layer GIN (300-d) initialised from GraphCL; text encoder: BERT-base initialised from SciBERT (MoMu-S) or KV-PLM (MoMu-K); 15,613 molecule-graph/document pairs; inter- and intra-modal InfoNCE. Downstream: PCdes retrieval (beats SMILES-based baselines), ChEBI-20 captioning by injecting MoMu graph features into MolT5's encoder (improves BLEU/METEOR/Text2Mol but *lowers ROUGE-L*; no numbers in text), MoleculeNet (8 datasets, scaffold split; beats GraphCL on 6, comparable on SIDER, slightly worse on HIV), zero-shot text-to-graph generation via MoFlow. — [arXiv 2209.05481 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.05481); [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05481)
- **MoleculeSTM — "Multi-modal Molecule Structure-text Model for Text-based Retrieval and Editing"** (S. Liu, Nie, Wang, Lu, Qiao, L. Liu, Tang, Xiao, Anandkumar; arXiv 2022–2024; the journal version is in Nature Machine Intelligence 2023 — the Nature page redirected to a login wall and could not be read, so venue is taken from the assignment and is *unverified here*). Two structure branches: SMILES via MegaMolBART (pretrained on ~500M ZINC molecules) and 2D graph via GIN initialised from GraphMVP; text branch SciBERT; PubChemSTM ≈281K structure–text pairs / ≈250K molecules; contrastive (EBM-NCE/InfoNCE). MoleculeNet ROC-AUC (avg of 8): MegaMolBART 69.84 → MoleculeSTM-SMILES 73.33; GraphMVP 73.50 → MoleculeSTM-Graph 74.57. Per dataset (SMILES-branch / Graph-branch): BBBP 70.75/69.98, Tox21 75.71/76.91, ToxCast 65.17/65.05, SIDER 63.70/60.96, ClinTox 86.60/92.53, MUV 65.69/73.40, HIV 77.02/76.93, BACE 81.99/80.77. Zero-shot DrugBank retrieval: graph branch better on Description/Pharmacodynamics, SMILES branch slightly better on ATC. — [arXiv 2212.10789v3 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.10789v3)
- **GIT-Mol — "GIT-Mol: A Multi-modal Large Language Model for Molecular Science with Graph, Image, and Text"** (P. Liu, Ren, Tao, Z. Ren; Computers in Biology and Medicine 108073, 2024; arXiv Aug 2023). LM: MolT5 (base as decoder for captioning/generation; large as embedding layer for property prediction; SciBERT also named as text encoder in GIT-Former — the paper does not reconcile this); "GIT-Mol (700M)". Graph encoder: GIN from MoMu (frozen); image encoder: Swin from SwinOCSR; fusion: GIT-Former = BLIP-2-style Q-Former with per-modality cross-attention, trained with cross-modal matching (XTM) + contrastive (XTC). Data: ~4.8M PubChem compounds (images/SMILES/graphs), ~320K captions + ChEBI-20. Tasks: ChEBI-20 captioning (BLEU-4 0.263 vs MolT5-base 0.247), generation (validity 0.928 vs 0.772, but MolT5-base higher on BLEU/exact match), MoleculeNet Tox21/ToxCast/SIDER/ClinTox/BBBP/BACE (no HIV): GIT-Mol(G+S) avg AUC 74.90 vs Mole-BERT 72.58, GraphMVP 71.23, MoMu 71.1. **Modality ablation:** property prediction avg AUC SMILES-only 70.0, graph-only 69.1, graph+SMILES 74.90; captioning BLEU-4 SMILES-only 0.176, graph-only 0.210, both 0.263. — [arXiv 2308.06911v3 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.06911v3); [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06911)

**Generative LM + graph era (2023–2024)**

- **MolCA — "MolCA: Molecular Graph-Language Modeling with Cross-Modal Projector and Uni-Modal Adapter"** (Z. Liu, S. Li, Luo, Fei, Cao, Kawaguchi, X. Wang, Chua; EMNLP 2023 main, per arXiv comment). Graph encoder: 5-layer GINE contrastively pretrained on 2M ZINC15 molecules; LM: Galactica-125M and Galactica-1.3B (MolT5-Large in one variant); fusion: Q-Former initialised from SciBERT with 8 learnable queries → soft tokens prepended to LM, SMILES also in the prompt; LoRA adapter on the LM. Pretraining PubChem324k (≈324K pairs; 12K/1K/2K high-quality split). Tasks: PubChem324k & ChEBI-20 captioning, IUPAC name prediction, molecule–text retrieval (PubChem324k/PCDes/MoMu), MoleculeNet (6 datasets), functional-group counting. Captioning PubChem324k (Galactica-1.3B): BLEU-2 38.7 vs MolT5-Large 30.2; ChEBI-20 BLEU-2 62.0. Retrieval M2T acc 66.6% vs MoleculeSTM 45.8%. **Same-LM SMILES-only ablation (Table 5a, Galactica-1.3B):** PubChem324k captioning 1D-SMILES-only BLEU-2 34.6 / 2D-graph-only 34.5 / SMILES+graph 38.7; ChEBI-20 55.3 → 62.0; IUPAC 71.0 → 75.0. **MoleculeNet ROC-AUC mean of 6: 72.1 (SMILES only) → 74.0 (SMILES+graph); ToxCast 56.2→64.5, SIDER and Tox21 improve, BBBP drops 70.8→70.0, BACE and ClinTox differences are within reported std.** Linear projector (35.2 BLEU-2) barely beats SMILES-only (33.7) whereas Q-Former reaches 39.8. — [arXiv 2310.12798 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12798); [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12798)
- **3D-MoLM — "Towards 3D Molecule-Text Interpretation in Language Models"** (S. Li, Z. Liu, Luo, X. Wang, He, Kawaguchi, Chua, Tian; ICLR 2024 — confirmed by ICLR proceedings page and the authors' GitHub "ICLR'24"). 3D encoder: Uni-Mol (pretrained on 209M conformations, frozen); projector: Q-Former (SciBERT-init, 8 queries); LM: Llama-2-7B with LoRA (r=8); the LM receives 3D tokens + 1D SMILES + instruction. Data: PubChem 316K 3D molecule–text pairs (RDKit MMFF conformers, GPT-3.5-enriched text), 3D-MoIT instruction set (PubChem computed properties; PubChemQC HOMO/LUMO/gap/SCF, ≈3.1M molecules, scaffold split). QM9 not used. Baselines include Llama-2-7B with SMILES only and **2D-MoLM** (same pipeline with a 2D graph encoder). Captioning (generalist, BLEU-2/ROUGE-L/METEOR): 3D-MoLM 28.95/30.02/33.55 vs Llama-2-7B SMILES-only 27.01/28.88/32.11 vs 2D-MoLM 27.15/29.12/32.28. Computed-property QA MAE (specialist; 3D-MoLM vs Llama-2-7B / 2D-MoLM / Uni-Mol): HOMO 0.26 vs 1.24/0.92/0.32; LUMO 0.25 vs 1.04/0.80/0.35; gap 0.28 vs 0.88/0.67/0.21; MW 14.79 vs 22.10/21.48/20.35. Retrieval M2T 69.05%. Null/negative: Uni-Mol (non-LM) beats 3D-MoLM on LogP and HOMO-LUMO gap; 3D-only prompting (no SMILES) does not beat Llama-2-7B on ROUGE-1/2; GPT-3.5 text enrichment lowered retrieval (72.06% → 69.05%). — [arXiv 2401.13923 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.13923); [ICLR 2024 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/4af24e6ce753c181e703f3f0be3b5e20-Abstract-Conference.html); [GitHub](https://github.com/lsh0520/3D-MoLM)
- **InstructMol — "InstructMol: Multi-Modal Integration for Building a Versatile and Reliable Molecular Assistant in Drug Discovery"** (Cao, Z. Liu, Lu, Yao, Y. Li; arXiv Nov 2023; COLING 2025 per search result/ACL Anthology). Graph encoder: 5-layer GIN (300-d, ≈1.8M params) from MoleculeSTM, frozen; LM: Vicuna-v1.3-7B with LoRA (r=64); projector: single linear layer; sequence input is **SELFIES**, not SMILES; graph tokens and SELFIES tokens concatenated. Stage 1: ≈264K PubChem pairs (projector only); Stage 2: instruction tuning. Tasks: ChEBI-20 description; QM9 HOMO/LUMO/gap regression (Mol-Instructions); MoleculeNet BACE/BBBP/HIV classification (scaffold); forward/retro/reagent prediction (USPTO via Mol-Instructions). **Graph-only (InstructMol-G) vs graph+SELFIES (GS):** QM9 MAE HOMO 0.0060→0.0048, LUMO 0.0070→0.0050, gap 0.0082→0.0061; BACE 84.3→82.1, BBBP 68.6→72.4, HIV 74.0→68.9; forward-reaction exact 0.153→0.536; retrosynthesis 0.114→0.407. No clean "text-only Vicuna" ablation is reported in the tables read. Ablation: MLP vs linear ≈+1%; 13B LLM gives minor gains; replacing GIN with GraphMVP drops BLEU 3–5%; InstructMol trails a GIN specialist on HIV. — [arXiv 2311.16208v2 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.16208v2); [COLING 2025 PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.25.pdf); [project page](https://idea-xl.github.io/InstructMol)
- **MolX — "MolX: Enhancing Large Language Models for Molecular Understanding With A Multi-Modal Extension"** (Le, Chawla et al.; Notre Dame/UCLA; page lists ACM SIGKDD 2025 Toronto). LM: Llama-2-7B (Mistral-7B in ablation), frozen during pretraining; encoders: ChemBERTa SMILES encoder, ChemGraphCL GIN graph encoder, Morgan fingerprint (radius 2); fusion: SMILES and graph embeddings averaged, fingerprint added with trainable weights → a single soft token. Pretraining ≈300K PubChem pairs + 10 auxiliary low-level property tasks + SMILES canonicalisation; adds 0.53% trainable params. Tasks: PubChem description/IUPAC generation; MoleculeNet ESOL/FreeSolv/Lipophilicity/HIV/BACE/BBBP/Tox21. Description (LoRA FT) BLEU-2 27.54 (Llama-2-7B alone) → 31.40 (MolX). **Ablation does not isolate the graph encoder**: "w/o ChemInit" removes pretrained weights of both encoders; removing the Morgan fingerprint costs more (BLEU-2 31.40→29.33) than removing the weighting scheme (→31.13). Property prediction is mixed: FreeSolv inference-only RMSE 359.69 worse than base LLM 357.37; MolX behind ChemGraphMVP on FreeSolv/Lipophilicity (LoRA). — [arXiv 2406.06777 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06777); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.06777)
- **UniMoT — "UniMoT: Unified Molecule-Text Language Model with Discrete Token Representation"** (v1 authors Zhang, Bian, Chen, Yao; v2 (June 2025) authors Guo, Bian, Wang, Yin, Z. Wang, Yao; arXiv, venue not stated). LM: Llama-2-7B + LoRA; molecule encoder: frozen MoleculeSTM encoder (300-d); fusion: causal Q-Former (16 queries) → vector-quantised tokenizer (codebook K=2048) whose codes are added to the LLM vocabulary with [MOL]/[/MOL]; ChemFormer SMILES encoder/decoder used for reconstruction. Tasks: 8 MoleculeNet classification sets + QM9 regression; PubChem/ChEBI-20 captioning; retrieval; Mol-Instructions generation. ROC-AUC: BACE 83.69 (InstructMol 82.30), BBBP 71.37 (70.00), HIV 78.49 (GraphMVP 77.74), Tox21 76.43 (GraphMVP 77.06 — UniMoT not best); QM9 avg MAE 0.0049 vs InstructMol 0.0050. Captioning PubChem BLEU-4 23.8 vs MolCA(1.3B) 21.3. **No SMILES-only ablation of the same LLM is reported** in the content read; continuous Q-Former embeddings (BLEU-4 25.2) beat discrete tokens (23.8), a cost the authors accept for unification. — [arXiv 2408.00863v2 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00863v2); [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00863)
- **LLaMo — "LLaMo: Large Language Model-based Molecular Graph Assistant"** (J. Park, Bae, Ko, H. J. Kim; NeurIPS 2024). LM: Llama-2-7b-chat (generalist) / Galactica-1.3B (specialist, matching MolCA); graph encoder: 5-layer GIN from MoleculeSTM; fusion: multi-level graph projector (MGProj) — learnable queries cross-attend to node features from *every* GNN layer plus motif tokens (functional groups) → b·(L+2) graph tokens; LM also receives SMILES. Stage 2 data includes ≈12K GPT-4 multi-turn conversations. Tasks: Mol-Instructions description, PubChem324k IUPAC, QM9 HOMO/LUMO/gap (MAE), reaction tasks. Generalist: description BLEU 38.9 vs GPT-4 (4-shot) 27.0; property MAE 0.006 vs Mol-Instructions 0.013 vs GPT-4 0.019. Specialist vs MolCA (Galactica-1.3B): PubChem324kV2 BLEU 34.4 vs 30.3; ChEBI-20 54.8 vs 53.1. **Projector ablation (Table 4): "No graph" (SMILES only) description BLEU 26.1/METEOR 56.6, IUPAC BLEU 36.3, property MAE 0.013 → MLP single-layer 32.4–33.8 / MAE 0.008–0.009 → full MGProj 37.8/66.1, IUPAC 49.6, MAE 0.007.** Negative: LLaMo below InstructMol-GS on retrosynthesis exact match (0.341 vs 0.407) and forward BLEU. — [arXiv 2411.00871v1 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00871v1); [NeurIPS 2024 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/94836); [GitHub](https://github.com/mlvlab/LLaMo)

**2025–2026 follow-ups**

- **Mol-LLM — "Mol-LLM: Multimodal Generalist Molecular LLM with Improved Graph Utilization"** (arXiv 2502.02810, v2 May 2025; NeurIPS 2025 per neurips.cc listing). LM: Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 + LoRA (r=64), SELFIES vocabulary from BioT5+; graph encoder: hybrid GINE + TokenGT (5 layers, 1024-d), pretrained with functional-group prediction (72 RDKit groups, 5M PubChem molecules) + SELFIES reconstruction; fusion: 5-layer SciBERT-init Q-Former with 32 queries → 32 tokens concatenated with instruction + SELFIES. New objective MolPO: SimPO-style preference between the true graph and a MACCS-perturbed graph. ≈3.3M train / 40K test instances over 21 tasks (MoleculeNet BACE/BBBP/ClinTox/HIV/SIDER, ESOL, Lipo, HOMO/LUMO/gap, Mol-Instructions & SMolInstruct reactions, ChEBI-20). **Same-model "w/o Graph" control:** LogS RMSE 1.36→1.28, LogD 0.95→0.91, forward-synthesis exact 0.893→0.911, retrosynthesis 0.510→0.538, reagent 0.202→0.225 (graph helps); **but MoleculeNet AUC BACE 80.8→80.5, BBBP 84.3→81.1, ClinTox 85.0→82.4, HIV 76.5→75.1 (w/o graph is better on most), ChEBI-20 ROUGE-L 0.509→0.439 (w/o graph better).** Authors attribute the classification null result to small MoleculeNet molecules the LLM can read from SELFIES. OOD: AqSol RMSE 1.02 (full) vs 1.10 (w/o graph) vs 1.32 (LlaSMol); ORDerly retrosynthesis 0.738 vs 0.727 vs 0.473 — most of the OOD advantage is already present without the graph. — [arXiv 2502.02810v2 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02810v2); [NeurIPS 2025 listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/125816)
- **EDT-Former — "Entropy-Guided Dynamic Tokens for Graph–LLM Alignment in Molecular Understanding"** (Jing, Zeng, Fang, Sun, B. Wang, Hu; Western University; arXiv 2602.02742v3, Mar 2026). LM: frozen Llama-3.1-8B (also Mistral-8B, Qwen3-8B, Llama2-7B); frozen graph encoders (names truncated in the portion read); a SMILES next-atom predictor's entropy peaks define segments; segment-pooled node embeddings become dynamic query tokens refined by a Dynamic Query Transformer; trained on Mol-LLaMA-Instruct. Tasks: zero-shot property prediction on 10 MoleculeNet/TDC sets (PAMPA, BBBP, BACE, ClinTox, DILI, hERG, HIA, HIV, AMES, PGP), MoleculeQA, Mol-Instructions. **Modality ablation on MoleculeQA (total score): Text-only SMILES 58.77; Text+3D 60.48; Text+Graph 65.11; Text+Graph+3D 68.34** — "graph information is essential while 3D provides complementary gains". — [arXiv 2602.02742 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02742)
- **MolBasic — "Back to Basics: Improving Molecular Understanding in LLMs via SMILES–Graph Translation"** (W. Wang, J. Feng, Z. Wei; Renmin Univ.; arXiv 2607.03007v1, July 2026). Text-only (no graph encoder): Qwen3-8B + LoRA trained to translate canonical SMILES ↔ textual adjacency list plus counting tasks ("structure-first"). MSC benchmark (2,316 PubChemSTM test molecules): all tested LLMs (Qwen3-8B, InternLM3-8B, ChatGLM3-6B, Vicuna-7B, T5-Large, MolT5-Large, ChemLLM-7B) score 0% on SMILES→graph and graph→SMILES conversion (ChemLLM 0.04%); heavy-atom counting 0.22–14.81%. After training: SMILES→graph 94.65%, heavy-atom counting 42.44%, substructure recognition 98.14%. Downstream 100K-PubChem property regression (MW/LogP/TPSA/HBD/HBA/RB/QED) improves over LLM baselines but Chemprop (GNN) still has lower MAE on most properties; GPT-5 beats MolBasic on most counting tasks but scores 0% on both conversion tasks. — [arXiv 2607.03007v1 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.03007v1)
- **"Improving Molecular Property Prediction in Small Language Models Using Graph-based Tools"** (Bougiatiotis, Kelesis, Paliouras; NCSR Demokritos; arXiv 2607.13115v1, July 2026; 2nd Causal NeSy workshop). Frozen SLMs (Llama 3.2 3B, Qwen 2.5 3B, DeepSeek 16B-MoE/2.4B active) receive graph information *as text*: a GIN expert's label+confidence hint, a GNNExplainer top-10%-edge subgraph rendered as SMILES, and a self-written rationale. MUTAG (188 mols) and a 1,000-molecule Tox21 subset. SMILES-only → all-context accuracy: DeepSeek MUTAG 56.14→75.44, Tox21 38.50→67.00; Qwen 55.36→70.18, 47.50→68.50; Llama 3.2 47.37→47.37 (no gain), 48.50→63.50. GIN expert alone: 84.21 / 71.00 — no SLM matched it. — [arXiv 2607.13115v1 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13115v1)
- **M²LLM — "M²LLM: Multi-view Molecular Representation Learning with Large Language Models"** (Ju, Zheng, Koh, C. Wang, Pan; Griffith/Monash; IJCAI 2025 proceedings PDF exists at ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0827.pdf; arXiv 2508.08657). No GNN: an LLM (Galactica 6.7B/30B, LLaMA-3.1-8B, OpenAI text-embedding-3) embeds SMILES under structure/task prompts and generates executable "rules" features; weighted fusion + MLP. MoleculeNet (scaffold, 10 seeds): classification avg 79.3 (OpenAI-large) vs best baseline Uni-Mol 76.9; ClinTox 99.5; BACE 80.0 < Random Forest 85.0; ESOL RMSE 0.44 vs MoleBert 1.02; FreeSolv 2.01 vs GROVER 2.38. SMILES-only→multi-view: FreeSolv ≈4.3→2.0, ESOL ≈1.55→0.44, BBBP ≈69→77, BACE small gain, ClinTox already ≈99–100 (multi-view slightly *lower* for LLaMA-3.1-8B: 98.67 vs 99.68). — [arXiv 2508.08657 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08657); [IJCAI 2025 PDF](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0827.pdf)
- **"Benchmarking Large Language Models for Molecule Prediction Tasks"** (Zhong, Zhou, Mottin; arXiv 2403.05075, Mar 2024 — first author is Zhong, not "Qian"). LLMs alone are "subpar … compared to structure-aware ML models" on ogbg-molbace/bbbp/hiv/esol/freesolv/lipo; a "GNNTrio" that feeds LM embeddings of the LLM response as extra GNN (GCN/GIN) features yields "significant performance improvements". Numbers not retrieved (secondary summaries only). — [arXiv 2403.05075](https://web3.arxiv.org/abs/2403.05075); [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2403.05075)

### Inferences
- Across the catalogue, the *same* graph encoder (MoleculeSTM/GraphCL 5-layer GIN, hidden 300) is reused by InstructMol, LLaMo, UniMoT and (via MoMu) GIT-Mol; differences in reported gains therefore come mostly from the projector (Q-Former / multi-level cross-attention vs linear) and from training recipe, not from graph-encoder capacity.
- The only paper with a 3D encoder (3D-MoLM) is also the only one with a controlled 2D-vs-3D arm (2D-MoLM), and it shows the 2D graph adds only ≈0.1–0.3 BLEU-2 over SMILES-only captioning while 3D adds ≈2 points and large MAE reductions on quantum properties.

## Key Question A2 — Which molecule papers show the 2D graph adds information beyond SMILES for an LM, which show it does not, and does any paper argue the 2D graph is redundant with SMILES?

### Takeaway
Evidence is genuinely split and task-dependent: generation-style tasks (captioning, IUPAC, reaction prediction, QM9 regression) consistently show same-LM gains from adding graph tokens (MolCA, LLaMo, Mol-LLM, GIT-Mol, EDT-Former), while MoleculeNet classification gains are small, uneven, or negative (MolCA BBBP −0.8 and within-std on BACE/ClinTox; Mol-LLM's no-graph variant *better* on BACE/BBBP/ClinTox/HIV). Two 2025–2026 papers explicitly argue redundancy/under-use: Mol-LLM documents a "graph bypass" phenomenon under naive SFT, and Ariguib–Niepert–Manolache state that "SMILES and 2D molecular graphs encode largely redundant information".

### Cited Findings

**Positive (graph adds beyond the string, same LM):**
- MolCA, Galactica-1.3B, same pipeline: captioning BLEU-2 34.6 (SMILES only) → 38.7 (SMILES+graph); IUPAC 71.0 → 75.0; MoleculeNet mean AUC 72.1 → 74.0, driven by ToxCast (56.2→64.5), SIDER, Tox21. — [MolCA HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12798)
- LLaMo "No graph" vs full projector: description BLEU 26.1 → 37.8; IUPAC BLEU 36.3 → 49.6; QM9 MAE 0.013 → 0.007 (Llama-2-7b-chat). — [LLaMo HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00871v1)
- GIT-Mol: SMILES-only 70.0 / graph-only 69.1 / both 74.90 avg AUC (6 MoleculeNet sets). — [GIT-Mol HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.06911v3)
- EDT-Former MoleculeQA: SMILES-only 58.77 → +graph 65.11 → +graph+3D 68.34. — [EDT-Former HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02742)
- Mol-LLM "w/o Graph" control: regression (LogS 1.36→1.28, LogD 0.95→0.91) and reaction exact-match (+1.8 to +2.8 pts) improve with the graph. — [Mol-LLM HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02810v2)
- 3D-MoLM: 2D-MoLM beats Llama-2-7B SMILES-only on descriptive QA BLEU-2 (30.84 vs 28.15) and on computed-property MAE (HOMO 0.92 vs 1.24), but 3D-MoLM beats both (0.26). — [3D-MoLM HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.13923)
- Graph-derived *text* (GIN hint + explainer subgraph) lifts frozen 3B SLMs by 27–74% relative on MUTAG/Tox21 — but one model (Llama 3.2) gets zero gain on MUTAG. — [arXiv 2607.13115](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13115v1)

**Null / negative (graph adds little or hurts):**
- MolCA: BBBP 70.8 → 70.0 with graph; BACE and ClinTox differences within std; graph-only ≈ SMILES-only on captioning (34.5 vs 34.6). — [MolCA HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12798)
- Mol-LLM (Mistral-7B): w/o-graph variant better on BACE (80.8 vs 80.5), BBBP (84.3 vs 81.1), ClinTox (85.0 vs 82.4), HIV (76.5 vs 75.1) and ChEBI-20 ROUGE-L (0.509 vs 0.439); the authors' Figure-1 "graph discrimination ratio" shows the SFT model "hardly distinguishes" true vs MACCS-perturbed graphs ("graph bypass"), motivating MolPO. — [Mol-LLM HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02810v2)
- 3D-MoLM: 2D-MoLM vs Llama-2-7B SMILES-only generalist captioning 27.15 vs 27.01 BLEU-2 — essentially no 2D gain. — [3D-MoLM HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.13923)
- InstructMol: adding SELFIES to graph tokens hurts BACE (84.3→82.1) and HIV (74.0→68.9) but helps BBBP (68.6→72.4) — mixed; and MolX's ablation never removes the graph encoder alone. — [InstructMol HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.16208v2); [MolX HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06777)
- M²LLM: SMILES-only LLM embeddings are already near ceiling on ClinTox (≈99–100 AUC), with multi-view slightly lower for LLaMA-3.1-8B; big gains only on regression (FreeSolv/ESOL) and BBBP. — [M²LLM HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08657)

**Explicit redundancy arguments:**
- "SMILES and 2D molecular graphs encode largely redundant information," citing the supervised multimodal setting of Manolache, Tantaru & Niepert, *MolMix* (NeurIPS 2024 MLSB workshop) where "the 1D modality contributed least to overall performance"; the authors therefore restrict their framework to 2D and 3D. — [Ariguib, Niepert, Manolache, "Learning the Neighborhood", arXiv 2509.22468, App. A.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.22468)
- MolBasic: LLMs trained on SMILES "fail to capture fundamental graph structure information" (0% SMILES↔graph conversion for all 7 tested LLMs and GPT-5), yet the proposed fix is *text-only* translation training rather than a graph encoder; no head-to-head with graph-encoder models. — [arXiv 2607.03007](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.03007v1)
- MoleculeSTM (pre-LLM, contrastive): SMILES branch and graph branch both gain from text alignment, graph branch avg 74.57 vs SMILES branch 73.33 (8 MoleculeNet sets) — a 1.2-point gap between modalities under identical text supervision. — [MoleculeSTM HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.10789v3)

### Inferences
- The clearest pattern: when the LM must *produce long structured outputs* (IUPAC names, reactions, captions) or regress continuous quantum properties, graph tokens help; when the task is a small-data binary classification on short MoleculeNet molecules, a 7B LM reading SMILES/SELFIES already saturates what a frozen 2D GIN can add, which is exactly Mol-LLM's stated explanation.
- The "graph bypass" finding (Mol-LLM) plus GraphToken-style "token collapse" results in the graph-reasoning literature (see B2) suggest that reported gains from graph soft tokens should be checked with perturbation controls, since next-token SFT can ignore the graph channel when the string channel suffices.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that runs a *controlled* SMILES-only vs SMILES+graph ablation across all of BACE/BBBP/HIV/Tox21 with multiple seeds and significance tests; MolCA (6 sets, 1 comparison) and Mol-LLM (5 sets) are the closest.
- MolMix (Manolache et al. 2024) itself was not read; the "1D contributed least" claim is reported second-hand via arXiv 2509.22468.
- No molecule paper was found that varies *LM scale* while holding the graph encoder fixed to show graph benefit as a function of LM size (InstructMol's 7B→13B ablation is graph-only and reports "minor gains").

## Key Question B1 — Catalogue of LLM graph-reasoning benchmarks (GraphQA / Talk like a Graph, NLGraph, GraphInstruct ×2, GraphArena, GraphWiz, GraphLLM, GraphToken, Huang et al., LLM4DyG, 2025–2026 follow-ups)

### Takeaway
Benchmarks from 2023–2024 (NLGraph, GraphQA, GraphInstruct, GraphWiz, LLM4DyG) use NetworkX-style synthetic Erdős–Rényi/BA/SBM graphs of 5–35 nodes (GraphWiz up to 100) serialized as edge lists or natural-language sentences, and find that prompted LLMs barely beat majority baselines on degree/edge-existence and collapse with graph size; 2024–2026 benchmarks (GraphArena, GraphOmni, GraphInstruct v4, GraphQA-Hardened in the Oracle serialization study, GraphInfer-Bench) move to real graphs, 30–75 nodes, NP-hard tasks and 10–15 serialization formats, and report format-induced accuracy swings of up to 28–40 points for the same model.

### Inferences
- Across NLGraph → GraphQA → GraphInstruct → GraphArena → GraphOmni, the headline failure modes are stable: counting/degree tasks fail with text serialization, accuracy degrades monotonically with node count, and models exploit structural priors (cycles exist; nodes are disconnected). Only the two papers that *replace* text with a learned structure prefix (GraphLLM, GraphToken) report near-ceiling accuracy on counting/degree — and only on ≤20–45-node synthetic graphs.
- Benchmarks that include a GNN arm (GraphArena, Sanford et al., GraphInfer-Bench) consistently find a plain GNN competitive with or better than LLM-based methods on large/NP or multi-node tasks, which bounds the value of the LLM for pure structure computation.

## Key Question B2 — Serialized text vs. GNN/graph-token encoder feeding the LLM: how do they compare, when do structure encoders help, and what token costs does serialization incur as graphs grow?

### Takeaway
Direct comparisons give three-way evidence: (1) learned structure prefixes beat serialized text by large margins on synthetic counting/degree/path tasks and cut context by ~96% (GraphLLM: 40–55 tokens vs 1.2K–3.3K; GraphToken: +10 to +72 pts on GraphQA); (2) on text-attributed real graphs, 2025–2026 audits find graph tokens often add nothing beyond the verbalized text (constant-prompt baseline ≥ G-Retriever on 3 benchmarks; soft-prompt LLM ≈ GraphToken/TEA-GLM on CLeGR and Arxiv) unless the dataset is "structurally sufficient" (Cora: 28.69 soft prompt vs 86.72 GraphToken); (3) serialization token cost grows roughly with edge count and format verbosity (adjacency matrix worst, JSON-LD/Turtle ≈1.7–4× edge-list), pushing 45–100-node graphs past 4K-token windows, whereas graph-token methods use a fixed budget (5 tokens GraphLLM; 32 queries Mol-LLM; 128-token blocks AGT).

### Cited Findings

**Head-to-head, structure encoder wins:**
- GraphLLM (LLaMA 2 7B): avg 98.19% vs ≈47.35% for the best fine-tuned Graph2Text baseline; context ≈40–55 tokens vs 1.2K–1.8K (zero-shot/LoRA/prefix) and 2.5K–3.3K (few-shot/CoT) → **96.45% average context reduction**; at 45 nodes Graph2Text LLaMA-2 exceeds 4,096 tokens and gpt-3.5-turbo-16k falls to 0 while GraphLLM keeps ≈96%. — [arXiv 2310.05845](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05845)
- GraphToken (frozen Flan-PaLM 2 S): node count 0.996 vs 0.276, node degree 0.962 vs 0.292, edge count 0.426 vs 0.128 (vs best text prompting); encoder body only 17k–199k params. No token-cost analysis given. — [arXiv 2402.05862](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05862)
- Agentic Graph Token Reasoning (Qwen2.5-3B/7B; 2-layer GraphSAGE, 128-token block per action): zero-shot transfer average 57.4% (graph-token medium) vs 28.1% (verbalised-text medium) over PubMed/ogbn-products/CiteSeer/STRING-db with identical agent loop; in-domain 81.0% vs 77.8%; budget ≈900 tokens/query (4 actions) vs ≈300 for single-pass graph-token baselines and ≈790–1,430 (>1,600 on heavy queries) for text-retrieving AgentGL. — [arXiv 2608.00542v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00542v1)
- Sanford et al., "Understanding Transformer Reasoning Capabilities via Graph Algorithms" (Sanford, Fatemi, Hall, Tsitsulin, Kazemi, Halcrow, Perozzi, Mirrokni; arXiv 2405.18512, May 2024; venue not stated on page): trained-from-scratch ≤60M transformers vs GCN/MPNN/GIN on GraphQA at 1K vs 100K samples — **GNNs win in the low-data (1K) regime and on local tasks (node degree: MPNN 99.4 vs transformer 31.6 at 1K), transformers win at 100K and on global/parallelizable tasks (connectivity 98.0 vs GCN 83.8, MPNN 94.4; shortest path 97.1 vs 66.8–72.6)**; fine-tuned T5-11B reaches 98.4 connectivity with only 1K samples; best prompting of large LLMs is far below (connectivity 84.9, shortest path 40.4, node degree 29.2, triangle counting 12.7). — [arXiv 2405.18512 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18512); [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18512)

**Head-to-head, structure encoder adds little or loses:**
- Token Collapse (Di Francesco, Jain, Nardini, Tonellotto, Liò, Silvestri; arXiv 2610.31572, Oct 2026): G-Retriever's graph tokens are near-identical across inputs (cosine 0.985–0.998); a learned **constant prompt with no graph access matches/exceeds G-Retriever** (ExplaGraphs 85.56 vs 85.56; SceneGraphs 81.40 vs 81.35; WebQSP Hit@1 71.25 vs 70.33; frozen Llama-2-7B); swapping/corrupting the token's graph content changes nothing beyond a 2-point threshold while removing the verbalized graph costs 19–51 points; collapse also found in GRAG, GRETPool, GraSP. — [arXiv 2610.31572 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31572)
- CLeGR / "A Graph Talks, But Who's Listening? Rethinking Evaluations for Graph-Language Models" (Petkar, Aakash K, Vempati, Sinha, Kumaraguru, Agarwal; arXiv 2508.20583, Aug 2025): synthetic subway graphs (≈1,000 graphs; 22K retrieval + 32K reasoning questions); GLMs TEA-GLM, GraphToken (GraphSAGE/GAT), G-Retriever on Llama3-8B / Phi3-3.5B; **soft-prompted LLM (no graph encoder, graph given as text) performs on par with GLMs on CLeGR, including zero-shot transfer and 3× larger graphs; on Arxiv Llama3-8B-SPT 76.03 vs GraphToken 75.49; but on "structurally sufficient" Cora SPT 28.69 vs GraphToken 86.72** (linear probe on frozen graph tokens ≈ full GLM, r=0.9643); on Computers/Photo plain GNNs beat GLMs; conclusion: GLMs "fail to leverage their structural encoders for graph reasoning tasks". — [arXiv 2508.20583 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20583)
- GraphArena: GraphToken improved over its Gemma-7b backbone but **underperformed a standalone GIN**; GNNs beat Claude-3.5-Sonnet on large NP tasks; adjacency-matrix serialization worst (0.436 hallucination, large shortest distance). — [arXiv 2407.00379v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00379v2)
- GraphInfer-Bench: seven graph-token models and Graph2Text SFT all plateau ≈0.3 NMI on community detection vs GraphSAGE ≈0.46. — [arXiv 2606.11562](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11562)
- Huang et al.: for prompted ChatGPT, removing structural wording (linearization) changes accuracy ≤0.2 pts and rewiring changes ≈−0.25% (vs −5.55% MPNN) — i.e., text serialization of neighborhoods is consumed as text, not structure. — [arXiv 2309.16595](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.16595)

**Token cost of serialization as graphs grow:**
- GraphWiz caps graphs at 100 nodes "to fit typical 4096-token input limits"; shortest-path accuracy → ≈0 beyond ≈60 nodes. — [arXiv 2402.16029v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16029v2)
- GraphArena problems reach ≈6,000 tokens; common-neighbor prompts average ≈4,324 characters (max 18,014); hallucination rises with node count (≈16% at 5 nodes → >80% at 30, GPT-4o diameter). — [arXiv 2407.00379v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00379v2)
- NLGraph could use only one in-context exemplar for GNN simulation "because of the context size limit". — [arXiv 2305.10037](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10037)
- Oracle serialization study: GraphQA prompts ≈1,008 (structured) to ≈4,608 (shacl_lite) tokens on gpt-5.4; turtle/jsonld costliest; a serialization selector adds +9.52 pts at 3.71× cost. — [arXiv 2610.22734](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22734)
- KG-LLM-Bench (second-hand): edge list ≈2,645 vs structured JSON ≈4,505 tokens for the same KG. — [arXiv 2504.07087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07087v1)
- GraphOmni: closed models answer in <300 output tokens, o4-mini >1.6K; format alone moves GPT-4o diameter accuracy by up to 40 pts. — [arXiv 2504.12764](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12764)
- Fixed-budget graph-token designs: GraphLLM 5 prefix tokens; Mol-LLM 32 Q-Former queries; MolCA/3D-MoLM 8 queries; UniMoT 16 queries; AGT 128 tokens per action. — [GraphLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05845); [Mol-LLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02810v2); [MolCA](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12798); [3D-MoLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.13923); [UniMoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00863v2); [AGT](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00542v1)

### Inferences
- The disagreement between (1) and (2) is explained by *where the task signal lives*: on synthetic graphs with no node text (GraphQA, GraphLLM tasks) the only information channel is structure, so a structure encoder is decisive; on text-attributed graphs (WebQSP, Arxiv, CLeGR) the verbalized node text already carries the answer and graph tokens collapse into a constant soft prompt. Cora/CiteSeer (sparse text, high homophily) are the exception where encoders win decisively.
- Token savings from graph encoders are real but only demonstrated up to ~45–50 nodes (GraphLLM) or on ego-subgraphs; no paper measured graph-token vs serialization cost on the 75-node GraphQA-Hardened or GraphArena-large regimes.

### Gaps
- GraphToken reports no token or latency numbers; its cost advantage is inferred, not measured.
- No 2025–2026 paper was found that puts a GraphToken/GraphLLM-style encoder *and* the 15-format serialization sweep on the same GraphQA-Hardened graphs; the Oracle study explicitly lists this as not evaluated.
- The CLeGR paper's exact CLeGR accuracy numbers are only in a figure (not in text read).

## Key Question C — Studies varying training-set size, LLM scale, or input token budget and reporting structure-encoder benefit as a function of those

### Takeaway
Only a handful of studies vary these axes explicitly: Sanford et al. (GNN vs transformer at 1K vs 100K samples), GraphWiz (reasoning-path volume), GraphArena (model scale and SFT on 10K problems), Talk-like-a-Graph (PaLM 2 XXS→L), Huang et al. (text richness as a proxy for information budget), and GraphLLM (node count up to the 4K-token wall); no molecule paper varies LM scale with a fixed graph encoder beyond InstructMol's 7B→13B note.

### Cited Findings
- **Training-set size:** at 1K GraphQA samples GNNs (MPNN 99.4 node degree; GIN 98.8 cycle check) beat a ≤60M transformer (31.6; 97.1); at 100K the transformer overtakes on connectivity (98.0), shortest path (97.1) and cycle check (98.0, except MPNN 100.0); fine-tuned T5-11B reaches 98.4 connectivity from 1K samples. — [Sanford et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18512)
- **Training-set size (text-only reasoning):** GraphWiz 1:1 → 1:5 reasoning paths per graph raises LLaMA2-7B 44.17%→55.08% but lowers Triangle Sum 47.00%→38.75%. — [GraphWiz](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16029v2)
- **Training-set size (SFT on serialized graphs):** Llama3-8b SFT on ≈10K GraphArena problems lifts poly-small accuracy 28.6%→54.7% (≈Llama3-70b zero-shot) and cuts hallucination 53.9%→17.3%, with small gains on large NP tasks. — [GraphArena](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00379v2)
- **Training-set size (molecule, text-only):** MolBasic 1.18M structure-translation samples lift SMILES→graph conversion from 0% to 94.65% in Qwen3-8B; removing the structure stage ("w/o Struct") degrades downstream property prediction. — [MolBasic](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.03007v1)
- **LLM scale:** larger PaLM 2 variants improve on most GraphQA tasks but none beats the majority baseline on edge existence. — [Talk like a Graph](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.04560). Llama3-70b vs 8b gap is "much wider" on GraphArena than on GSM8K/GPQA; GPT-4o vs 4o-mini gap smaller than the price gap. — [GraphArena](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00379v2). GraphLLM 13B is *not* better than 7B (98.90/93.92/96.19/99.34 vs 99.90/95.77/97.26/99.81). — [GraphLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05845). InstructMol 13B: "minor gains", BLEU-2 slightly drops. — [InstructMol](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.16208v2). Structure gain from prompts is larger for the weaker LLM: LLaMA-2-7B-chat +21.7% avg vs ChatGPT +2.78% (rich) / +5.44% (scarce). — [Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.16595). SLM graph-tool gains depend on model competence, not size (DeepSeek 2.4B-active gains most; Llama 3.2 3B zero on MUTAG). — [arXiv 2607.13115](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13115v1). GraphInfer-Bench: scaling SFT to 70B helps constrained outputs but hurts open-vocabulary description. — [GraphInfer-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11562)
- **Input token budget / graph size:** GraphLLM keeps ≈96% at 45 nodes while Graph2Text baselines exceed the 4,096-token limit; GraphWiz shortest path → 0 beyond ≈60 nodes; GraphOmni BFS/triangle counting collapse at 30–50 nodes; GraphArena hallucination 16%→80% from 5 to 30 nodes; serialization selector adds +9.52 pts at 3.71× token cost. — [GraphLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05845); [GraphWiz](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16029v2); [GraphOmni](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12764); [GraphArena](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00379v2); [Oracle study](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22734)
- **Information budget in the text channel:** structure prompts help ChatGPT +2.78% when node text is rich vs +5.44% when scarce; MPNNs +6.98% / +14.07%. — [Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.16595)
- **Molecule size as implicit budget:** Mol-LLM attributes zero graph benefit on MoleculeNet to small molecules readable from SELFIES, and shows graph gains on OOD AqSol/ORDerly; MolBasic reports errors grow with molecular size. — [Mol-LLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02810v2); [MolBasic](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.03007v1)

### Inferences
- The structure-encoder advantage is largest exactly where the text channel is weakest: low data (1K samples), weak/small LMs, sparse node text, or graphs large enough that serialization saturates the context. Conversely, with 100K samples, a frontier LLM, or short molecules, text/SMILES alone closes most of the gap.

### Gaps
- No study was found that sweeps *LLM scale × graph-encoder on/off* on molecule property prediction, nor one that sweeps *training-set size × graph-encoder on/off* for molecule LLMs (MolCA/LLaMo/Mol-LLM each report a single data regime).
- No study measures graph-token vs serialization accuracy *and* token cost jointly beyond 45 nodes.
